# Practice Plan: Individual Tactical Awareness and Game Reading

## Objective:
To develop players' capacity to understand the overall game situation, anticipate actions, and make smart individual decisions aligned with team tactics.

## Key Concepts:
*   **Scanning and Awareness:** Constantly observing the field, teammates, and opponents.
*   **Anticipation:** Predicting opponent moves and future play development.
*   **Decision-Making:** Choosing the best action based on game context.
*   **Positional Understanding:** Knowing one's role and responsibilities within the team shape.
*   **Reading Opponent Tendencies:** Identifying patterns in opponent play.

## Session Structure:

**1. Warm-up (15 mins):**
*   **"Scan and React" Dribbling Circuit:
    *   **Setup:** Players dribble in a grid. Coach provides visual cues (e.g., pointing, holding up colored cards) or auditory cues that dictate the next action: dribble to a specific cone, pass to a target player, or make a specific type of run.
    *   **Focus:** Training players to process visual and auditory information quickly and react appropriately.
*   **"What's Next?" Passing Pairs:
    *   **Setup:** Players in pairs, passing. After passing, the receiver must anticipate the passer's next move or the likely action of an imaginary defender.
    *   **Drill:** Receiver makes a run or adjusts position based on their anticipation, then receives a pass from a third player. Emphasis on reading the passer's intent.

**2. Skill Development (25 mins):
*   **"Decision Point" Possession Games (4v4 to 5v5):
    *   **Setup:** Small-sided games with specific "decision points" highlighted. For example, a defender receiving the ball under pressure, a midfielder receiving between the opponent's lines, or a forward receiving with their back to goal.
    *   **Focus:** Players must demonstrate good scanning habits and make the optimal individual decision (pass, dribble, hold, lay-off) based on the immediate game context. Coach pauses play to discuss decisions and alternatives.
*   **"Opponent Tendency" Identification Drill:
    *   **Setup:** Show short video clips of games or simulate specific opponent patterns (e.g., a common pressing trap, a favored attacking pathway).
    *   **Drill:** Players identify the opponent's tendency and then demonstrate the tactical response or counter-move their team should employ.

**3. Tactical Application (30 mins):
*   **"Awareness Zone" Game (6v6 or 7v7):
    *   **Setup:** Play a small-sided game. Designate specific "awareness zones" on the field (e.g., the "blind spot" behind a player, the "danger zone" in front of goal).
    *   **Drill:** Coach calls out when a "threat" or "opportunity" enters a player's awareness zone, requiring them to react based on their tactical understanding.
    *   **Focus:** Encourages players to develop peripheral vision and maintain constant awareness of their surroundings.
*   **"Guided Discovery" SSG:
    *   **Setup:** Play a regular SSG (e.g., 7v7).
    *   **Coaching Method:** Instead of giving direct instructions, the coach asks players guiding questions during play: "What do you see happening around you?" "What are your options?" "Where is the space you could exploit?" "What is the opponent's likely next move?"
    *   **Focus:** Promotes independent tactical thinking, self-analysis, and better game reading.

**4. Cool-down (10 mins):**
*   Light jogging and static stretching.
*   Player reflection: "What is one specific thing you will focus on in terms of your tactical awareness during our next game?"

**Coach’s Troubleshooting:**
*   **Players not scanning enough:** Implement mandatory "look up" prompts before receiving the ball. Reward players who make informed decisions after scanning. Use drills that require scanning before passing.
*   **Difficulty reading opponent patterns or game flow:** Use video analysis sessions to break down opponent tactics. During practice games, encourage players to "report back" on what they observe about the opponent's strategy.
*   **Poor individual decision-making:** Use drills that simplify options, then gradually increase complexity. Ask players to explain their reasoning for decisions, reinforcing the thought process.

**Age-Appropriate Language:**
*   Use terms like "tactical awareness," "game reading," "scanning," "anticipation," "decision-making," "situational awareness," "positional understanding," "opponent tendencies," and "game intelligence."
*   Emphasize that understanding the game and making smart choices is as crucial as technical skill for becoming a successful player.
