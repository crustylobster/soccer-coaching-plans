# U11 Individual Drills: First Touch & Control

This session focuses on the crucial skill of the first touch – receiving the ball cleanly and efficiently to set up the next move, whether it's a pass, dribble, or shot.

## Drill 1: Wall Pass & Receive

*   **Objective:** Improve receiving the ball cleanly from a pass and controlling it quickly.
*   **Setup:** Player stands about 5-10 yards from a wall or a partner.
*   **Instructions:**
    1.  Player passes the ball against the wall (or to their partner).
    2.  As the ball rebounds, the player focuses on their first touch to control it.
    3.  The first touch should be controlled, cushioning the ball to bring it close.
    4.  After controlling it, the player can dribble, pass again, or take a touch towards goal.
    5.  Vary the passes: firm, soft, angled. Encourage using different parts of the foot for control (inside, outside, laces).
*   **Coach’s Troubleshooting:**
    *   **Ball bounces away after pass:** Emphasize a soft "cushion" touch, using the inside of the foot and slightly drawing it back.
    *   **Player taking too many touches:** Remind them the goal of the first touch is to set up the *next* action. Encourage controlling it into a space where they can dribble or pass.
    *   **Using only one foot:** Have players practice receiving with alternating feet, or try to control with the weaker foot.

## Drill 2: "Receive and Turn"

*   **Objective:** Practice receiving the ball and immediately turning with it to face a different direction.
*   **Setup:** Set up two cones 10-15 yards apart. Player starts with a ball at one cone.
*   **Instructions:**
    1.  Player dribbles towards the second cone.
    2.  As the ball approaches the cone, the player uses a feint or a body turn (e.g., Cruyff turn, inside-outside cut) to change direction and protect the ball.
    3.  Encourage players to look up before receiving to know where they want to turn.
    4.  Can be done with a partner passing the ball to the player who then turns.
*   **Coach’s Troubleshooting:**
    *   **Ball gets stuck or too far when turning:** Focus on keeping the touch close to the body during the turn, using the foot that is *not* receiving the ball to pivot.
    *   **Turn is slow and predictable:** Encourage a sharp, decisive move and then an immediate acceleration with the ball.
    *   **Player not looking up:** Remind them to scan before the ball arrives to see the space they want to turn into.

## Drill 3: "Receive in Tight Space" (Rondo Variation)

*   **Objective:** Improve ability to receive and control the ball under pressure from defenders.
*   **Setup:** A small playing area (e.g., 10x10 yards) with 3 players inside and 1 defender outside. The 3 players try to keep possession by passing to each other.
*   **Instructions:**
    1.  Players inside the grid try to pass the ball to each other, keeping it away from the defender.
    2.  Encourage quick passes and movement to create passing lanes.
    3.  When a player receives the ball, they should try to control it with their first touch in a way that either protects it from the defender or sets up an easy pass to another teammate.
    4.  The defender tries to win the ball.
*   **Coach’s Troubleshooting:**
    *   **Players are tackled easily:** Remind them to use their body to shield the ball from the defender, show the defender away from the ball.
    *   **Passes are too slow or inaccurate:** Focus on firm, accurate passes. Encourage players to make a clear "target" with their foot for the pass.
    *   **Players are too static:** Remind them to move to get open, create angles, and support the player with the ball.

---

**Coach’s Troubleshooting:**
*   **Ball is consistently controlled too far away:** Remind players to use a softer, "drawing" touch with the inside of the foot to bring the ball closer.
*   **First touch is poor and leads to losing possession:** Focus on the technique of receiving – eyes on the ball, soft foot, absorbing the pace of the pass.
*   **Players waiting for the ball to stop before acting:** Emphasize that the first touch is the *start* of the next action, not the end. It should guide the ball into a useful position.

**Age-Appropriate Language:**
*   "Catch the ball with your foot."
*   "Cushion the pass."
*   "Move the ball where you want to go."
*   "Use your body to protect the ball."
*   "Look up before you get it."
