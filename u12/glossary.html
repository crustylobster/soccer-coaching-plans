U12 Soccer Glossary

Welcome to the U12 season! As you develop your game intelligence, you'll encounter new terms. This glossary helps explain them so you can understand tactics and communicate better on the field.

  • Angle of Support: The position a player takes to offer a passing option. Good angles make it easier for teammates to pass to you and protect the ball.

  • Attacking Third: The part of the field nearest to the opponent's goal, where attacks are typically made.

  • Back Four: The four defenders who typically play across the back line (two center backs, two fullbacks).

  • Backheel: Kicking the ball with the heel of your foot, often used for a quick, unexpected pass or touch.

  • Blocking: Using your body or positioning to prevent an opponent from advancing with the ball or receiving a pass.

  • Build-Up Play: The process of moving the ball from defense to attack through organized passing and movement.

  • Channel: The wide areas of the field, near the sidelines. Playing in channels can stretch the opponent's defense.

  • Chip (Pass/Shot): A softly lofted kick that goes over an opponent's legs or head.

  • Clean Sheet: When a team prevents the opponent from scoring any goals during a match.

  • Clearance: Kicking the ball away from your goal, usually with power, to relieve defensive pressure.

  • Close Control: Keeping the ball very near your feet while dribbling, making it difficult for opponents to tackle.

  • Combination Play: Two or more players passing the ball back and forth in quick succession to move past opponents or create an attacking opportunity.

  • Compactness: When a team stays close together, minimizing space between players and defensive/midfield lines to deny the opponent space.

  • Conditioned Game: A game where specific rules or restrictions are added to focus on particular skills or tactical elements.

  • Counter-Attack: A quick attack launched immediately after winning possession from the opponent, often catching them out of position.

  • Cover: A defensive principle where a player positions themselves behind a teammate who is pressuring the ball to provide support and intercept passes.

  • Create Space: Players moving intelligently to open up areas on the field for teammates to receive passes or make runs.

  • Cruyff Turn: A skill where a player fakes a pass or dribble in one direction, then quickly pulls the ball back with the sole of their foot in the opposite direction.

  • Cut Back: When a player dribbles towards the byline and then cuts the ball back inside towards the goal or a teammate.

  • Defensive Third: The part of the field closest to your own goal.

  • Depth (Attacking): Players positioning themselves at different "lines" or distances from the ball carrier to offer passing options and stretch the opposition.

  • Direct Play: Playing the ball forward quickly, often with long passes, rather than focusing on intricate short passing.

  • Disguise: Making a pass, shot, or dribble look like it's going one way before doing something else, to trick an opponent.

  • Double Team: When two defenders work together to pressure the player with the ball.

  • Dribble: Moving the ball forward while keeping it close to your feet.

  • Drop Ball: A method of restarting play when the ball goes out of play without any player being at fault, or after a stoppage for injury.

  • Driving Run: Dribbling forward with speed and purpose, often trying to break lines or create scoring chances.

  • Dynamic: Being active, agile, and constantly moving on or off the ball.

  • Eliminate: To beat an opponent with a dribble, feint, or pass.

  • End Line: The boundary line at the back of the field, behind the goals.

  • Enveloping Run: A run made by a player that "envelops" or moves around a teammate who has the ball, offering a forward passing option.

  • Face Up: When a player receives the ball with their body and eyes open, ready to see options on the field.

  • Fast Break: A quick transition from defense to attack.

  • Feint: A deceptive movement or fake to trick an opponent.

  • First Touch: Controlling the ball with the first contact after receiving a pass.

  • Flick: A quick, skillful touch of the ball, often with the outside of the foot or toe.

  • Formation: The arrangement of players on the field (e.g., 4-4-2, 3-5-2).

  • Forward Pass: A pass made in the direction of the opponent's goal.

  • Free Kick: A kick awarded for a foul or infringement of the rules.

  • Fullback: Defenders who play on the sides of the defense.

  • Furthest Player: The attacker who is furthest up the field, often looking to make runs behind the defense.

  • Game Intelligence: A player's ability to understand the game, read situations, and make smart decisions.

  • Gateway: A player who is typically a midfielder that connects the defense to the attack, often involved in starting build-up play.

  • Goal Kick: A restart awarded when the attacking team sends the ball over the defending team's end line.

  • Goal Side: Positioning oneself between the opponent's attacker and your own goal when defending.

  • Half-space: The area of the field between the central attacking players and the wide players (wingers/fullbacks).

  • Hang Time: The duration the ball stays in the air after being kicked high.

  • Header: Using your head to control or pass the ball.

  • High Press: Aggressively closing down the opponent in their own half to try and win the ball back quickly.

  • Holding Midfielder: A midfielder who plays deeper, often in front of the defense, to win possession and help start attacks.

  • Hybrid Press: A defensive tactic that combines elements of man-marking and zonal marking.

  • ID Check: A quick glance by a player to assess the positions of teammates, opponents, and space before receiving the ball.

  • Immediate Transition: Reacting instantly to changes in possession, whether to counter-attack or recover defensively.

  • In-swinging Cross: A pass from wide areas that curves towards the center of the goal.

  • Individual Skill: Techniques performed by a single player, such as dribbling, juggling, or feinting.

  • Interception: Cutting out an opponent's pass by getting between the passer and their intended receiver.

  • Inverted Fullback: A fullback who moves inside towards the center of the pitch when their team has possession.

  • Jockey: To defend by staying between the attacker and the goal, delaying their progress and forcing them into a less dangerous position without committing to a tackle.

  • Juggling: Keeping the ball in the air using any part of the body except hands and arms.

  • Key Pass: A pass that directly leads to a shot or goal.

  • Kickoff: The method of starting the game or restarting play after a goal or at the beginning of a half.

  • Killer Pass: Another term for a "key pass" or "through ball" that is very difficult for the defense to stop.

  • Leadership: Players taking initiative to communicate, organize, and motivate teammates.

  • Line of Support: The passing lane created by a teammate moving into a position to receive a pass.

  • Local Team: A team based in the same geographic area.

  • Locking Down: Achieving strong defensive organization and preventing opponents from scoring.

  • Look Up: To scan the field for options before receiving the ball or making a pass.

  • Man-Marking: Each defender is assigned to a specific opponent to track and prevent them from influencing the game.

  • Midfield Line: The players who operate in the central area of the pitch.

  • Overlap: When a teammate runs past the player with the ball to offer a passing option or exploit space.

  • Overload: Having more players in a certain area of the field than the opponent, creating a numerical advantage.

  • Out-swinging Cross: A pass from wide areas that curves away from the center of the goal.

  • Panic Button: A player who, when under pressure, makes a poor decision like kicking the ball away aimlessly.

  • Parallel Pass: A pass made sideways, parallel to the goal line.

  • Partner Pass: A pass played to a teammate who immediately passes it back.

  • Pass and Move: A fundamental tactic where a player passes the ball and then immediately moves to an open position to receive it back or support the play.

  • Pass into Space: A pass played into an open area of the field for a teammate to run onto.

  • Path of the Ball: The trajectory of the ball after it is kicked.

  • Penalty Area: The larger rectangular box in front of each goal.

  • Penalty Arc: The D-shaped area outside the penalty box, from which penalty kicks are taken.

  • Penalty Kick: A direct free kick taken from the penalty spot, awarded for certain fouls inside the penalty area.

  • Perimeter Play: Playing the ball along the boundaries of the field.

  • Phase of Play: A specific period or stage of the game (e.g., attacking phase, defensive phase).

  • Pitch: The soccer field.

  • Pivot: Turning with the ball, often using the non-kicking foot as a stable base.

  • Placement: Directing a shot or pass accurately to a specific spot.

  • Playmaker: A creative attacking midfielder or forward who is adept at creating scoring chances for teammates.

  • Pocket of Space: An area on the field that is not being marked by an opponent.

  • Position: A player's general area of the field where they usually play.

  • Positional Play: A tactical approach that emphasizes smart positioning and movement to create and exploit space and passing options.

  • Possession: Keeping control of the ball.

  • Post-Shot Awareness: What a player does immediately after taking a shot, whether it's tracking the ball or supporting the play.

  • Power Play: An attacking strategy that involves strong, direct play, often with many players pushing forward.

  • Press: Actively closing down an opponent with the ball to try and win it back.

  • Pressure: The act of closing down an opponent to deny them time and space.

  • Pressing Trap: Defensive positioning designed to force the opponent into a specific area where they can be outnumbered and pressured.

  • Primary Target: The first or main player a passer is looking for.

  • Progression: Moving the ball forward towards the opponent's goal.

  • Protective Dribble: Shielding the ball with the body and legs to prevent an opponent from tackling it.

  • Pull Back: A pass played across the face of goal or into space for a supporting player.

  • Punt: A long kick by the goalkeeper, usually from their hands, to clear the ball upfield.

  • Quick Feet: Moving the feet rapidly while dribbling to maintain close control and change direction effectively.

  • Quick Turn: A rapid change of direction with or without the ball.

  • Radius of Influence: The area on the field where a player is most effective and can influence the game.

  • Ramalho Turn: A skillful turn where a player uses the outside of their foot to drag the ball behind their standing leg.

  • Range of Passing: The distance a player can accurately pass the ball.

  • Rapid Transition: The quick shift from one phase of play (attack/defense) to another.

  • Receiving: Controlling the ball after it is passed to you.

  • Recovery Run: Sprinting back towards your own goal after losing possession to regain defensive shape.

  • Red Card: Dismissal from the game for serious foul play or misconduct.

  • Referee: The official who controls the game and enforces the rules.

  • Repositioning: Moving to a new, better position on the field to support the team.

  • Rest Defense: The defensive structure maintained by players who are further back in the team's shape when the ball is in possession in the attacking half.

  • Return Pass: Passing the ball back to the player who passed to you.

  • Rhythm: The tempo and flow of the game, or a player's ability to maintain a consistent pace.

  • Ride a Tackle: Successfully dribbling past an opponent attempting to tackle you.

  • Rondo: A possession drill where players pass the ball in a circle, trying to keep it away from one or two defenders in the middle.

  • Role: The specific responsibilities of a player based on their position and the team's tactics.

  • Root Pass: A central pass that goes through the "heart" of the field.

  • Round the Corner: Passing the ball to a teammate who is running towards the sideline or attacking space.

  • Run: Moving with or without the ball, especially to create space or offer a passing option.

  • Run Onto It: To move towards a pass to receive it.

  • Sacrifice: A player making a defensive or supporting run that might not directly involve the ball but is crucial for team shape.

  • Safe Pass: A pass that is unlikely to be intercepted, usually short and firm.

  • Sandwich Tackle: A tackle where a player tries to dispossess an opponent by trapping the ball between their feet.

  • Scan: To look around the field to assess the positions of teammates, opponents, and available space.

  • Scoring Zone: The area around the opponent's goal where scoring opportunities are most frequent.

  • Scratch Pass: A deceptive pass that looks like it's going one way but is skillfully redirected.

  • Screening: Using your body to shield the ball from an opponent.

  • Second Line of Attack: Midfielders or forwards making runs to support the main attacking players.

  • Seeding: Arranging players in a tactical formation.

  • Self-Correction: A player recognizing their own mistake and adjusting their technique or decision-making.

  • Send Off: When a player is dismissed from the game by the referee (receives a red card).

  • Set Piece: A restart situation like a free kick, corner kick, or throw-in.

  • Shape: The formation and positioning of the team or individual players on the field.

  • Shielding: Using the body to protect the ball from an opponent.

  • Shift: When defenders move together as a unit to maintain their shape and cover space.

  • Shinguards: Protective padding worn on the shins.

  • Ship Pass: A long, diagonal pass, often used to switch play from one side of the field to the other.

  • Shooting Lane: The line of sight and space available to shoot at goal.

  • Side-Back: A defender who plays on the flanks of the defense.

  • Sideline: The long boundary lines of the pitch.

  • Silence: When a player makes a run or a pass without verbal communication.

  • Simple Pass: A short, accurate pass.

  • Single Pivot: One midfielder who plays deep to help transition from defense to attack.

  • Sit: To drop back and defend compactly, usually after losing possession in the attacking half.

  • Sit Deep: When a team defends in their own half, allowing the opponent possession.

  • Skill: A specific technique or ability that a player possesses.

  • Slalom: Dribbling through a series of obstacles.

  • Slide Tackle: A defensive maneuver where a player slides along the ground to tackle the ball.

  • Slicing Pass: A pass that penetrates the opponent's defensive line.

  • Small-Sided Game: A game played with fewer players (e.g., 3v3, 5v5) on a smaller field, often used for development.

  • Smart Pass: A pass that is well-weighted, accurate, and into a position that helps the team.

  • Smother: To effectively block or contain an opponent.

  • Snap Pass: A quick, firm pass.

  • Soccer IQ: Same as Game Intelligence.

  • Sole (of the foot): Used for dribbling, trapping, and turning the ball.

  • Solo Run: A dribble where a player carries the ball a significant distance on their own.

  • Soprano Pass: A very soft pass that requires delicate control.

  • Sort Out: To organize or position oneself effectively.

  • Space: Open areas on the field where players can move or receive the ball.

  • Span: The width of the field or the distance covered by a pass.

  • Spark: A player with creativity and flair who can ignite attacks.

  • Speed Dribble: Dribbling the ball at a high pace.

  • Speed of Play: The tempo at which the ball and players move during a game.

  • Spin: To turn quickly with the ball.

  • Spiral: A well-executed pass or shot that has a spinning motion.

  • Split the Defense: To pass the ball through the opponent's defensive line to a teammate making a run.

  • Spot Pass: A precise pass aimed at a specific location.

  • Square Pass: A pass made sideways, parallel to the goal line.

  • Squeeze: To apply pressure to the opponent to reduce their space.

  • Staggered Runs: Teammates making runs at different depths or angles to offer multiple passing options.

  • Stand Tackle: A tackle made while standing up, using the feet and legs.

  • Stance: A player's body position when receiving, dribbling, or tackling.

  • Star Player: The most talented or influential player on a team.

  • Steal: To take the ball away from an opponent.

  • Step-over: A feint where a player quickly moves their leg over the ball without touching it, often to deceive an opponent.

  • Sticking: A defensive tactic of staying close to the player you are marking.

  • Stoppage: A break in play for fouls, out-of-bounds, or injuries.

  • Straight Pass: A pass made directly towards the target.

  • Stratagem: A plan or tactic for winning.

  • Strategy: The overall plan for how a team will play.

  • Stretch the Defense: To spread out the opponent's defensive line, creating more space in the middle.

  • Striker: An attacking player whose primary role is to score goals.

  • Strike Zone: The area on the ball a player contacts when shooting.

  • Strong Foot: The foot a player is more comfortable and skilled with.

  • Support: Positioning oneself to be available for a pass from a teammate.

  • Support Player: A player who moves to provide a passing option or assistance to a teammate with the ball.

  • Surface (of the foot): Different parts of the foot used for controlling and passing the ball (inside, outside, sole, laces).

  • Swerve: To kick the ball with spin, causing it to curve.

  • Switch of Play: Passing the ball from one flank of the field to the other to change the direction of attack.

  • System: The way a team is organized and plays tactically.

  • Tackle: An attempt to dispossess an opponent of the ball.

  • Tactical: Relating to the planning and execution of strategies in a game.

  • Tactical Discipline: Adhering to the team's tactical plan, especially in defense.

  • Tactical Foul: A foul committed deliberately to disrupt an opponent's attack or prevent a dangerous situation.

  • Tactical Nuance: Subtle aspects of strategy and positioning.

  • Tactical Retreat: Falling back into defensive positions when possession is lost.

  • Take-On: To attempt to dribble past an opponent.

  • Take-Off: The moment a player jumps or accelerates.

  • Talk: Verbal communication between teammates on the field.

  • Team Shape: The formation and relative positions of players when the team is attacking or defending.

  • Teamwork: Players working together towards a common goal.

  • Tempo: The speed at which the game is played.

  • Tennis Ball Drill: A drill where players receive a tennis ball thrown at them and must control it with their feet, improving first touch under pressure.

  • Territory: The areas of the field (defensive, middle, attacking).

  • The Channel: The area between the center and the sideline.

  • The Middle: The central part of the pitch.

  • The Turn: The act of changing direction with the ball.

  • Through Ball: A pass played into the space behind the opponent's defense for a teammate to run onto.

  • Tight Marking: Defending an opponent very closely.

  • Timeliness: Executing an action at the correct moment.

  • Timing: The precise moment an action is performed (e.g., making a run, attempting a tackle).

  • Toe Punt: Kicking the ball with the toe, usually for power but less control.

  • Toe Tap: A quick touch with the toe of the foot, usually for close dribbling or feinting.

  • Top Corner: The upper corner of the goal.

  • Torque: The rotational force applied when striking the ball.

  • Total Football: A tactical system where players are fluid and can interchange positions.

  • Touch: Contacting the ball with any part of the body.

  • Touchline: The sideline of the pitch.

  • Toughness: Physical and mental resilience.

  • Track Back: To run back into a defensive position after losing possession or following a run forward.

  • Traffic Cone: Used as markers for drills.

  • Trail Ball: A dribble where the ball is kept slightly behind the player.

  • Trail Run: A teammate making a run behind the player with the ball.

  • Training: The process of practicing and developing skills.

  • Transition: The change from one phase of play to another (attack to defense, defense to attack).

  • Trap: Controlling the ball with the first touch.

  • Treatment: Medical attention given to an injured player.

  • Triangular Passing: Passing the ball between three players to create a passing triangle.

  • Trigger: An event that initiates an action (e.g., a pass is a trigger for a run).

  • Trigger Run: A run made by a player when they see a potential passing opportunity.

  • Triple Threat: A player who can dribble, pass, or shoot effectively.

  • Trophy: An award for winning a competition.

  • Turn: Changing direction with the ball.

  • Turnover: Losing possession of the ball to the opponent.

  • Twinning: Two players combining using short, quick passes.

  • Two-Touch: Controlling the ball with the first touch and passing or shooting with the second.

  • U-Turn: A sharp turn that reverses direction.

  • Underlap: A teammate making a run inside the player with the ball to offer a passing option.

  • Underlap Run: A run made inside a teammate to receive a pass or exploit space.

  • Unmarked: A player who is not being closely defended by an opponent.

  • Urgency: Playing with speed and purpose, especially when transitioning or pressing.

  • Use of Body: Shielding the ball with the body to protect it from an opponent.

  • V-Cut: A sharp change of direction with the ball.

  • Vanishing Foam: Spray used by referees to mark the spot for free kicks.

  • Variation: Changing the type of drill, pass, or move to keep training interesting and challenging.

  • Vary the Pace: Changing the speed of dribbling or passing.

  • Vary the Tempo: Changing the speed at which the game is played.

  • Veer: To change direction suddenly.

  • Velocity: The speed and direction of movement.

  • Vertical Pass: A pass made forward, towards the opponent's goal.

  • View: A player's awareness of the field and options.

  • Vision: A player's ability to see and anticipate the game, making intelligent decisions.

  • Volley: Striking the ball in the air before it bounces.

  • Volume of Play: How often a player is involved in the game.

  • Vulnerability: A weakness in a team's or player's game that an opponent can exploit.

  • W-M Formation: A traditional attacking formation.

  • Wall Pass: Passing the ball to a teammate or wall and receiving it back after moving into space.

  • Warm-up: Physical activity to prepare the body for exercise.

  • Warped Pass: A pass that is curved or swerves.

  • Watch the Ball: Focusing attention on the ball, especially when receiving or shooting.

  • Weak Foot: The foot a player is less skilled with.

  • Wear Down: To tire out opponents through constant pressure or play.

  • Weight of Pass: The power or pace of a pass, suited to the receiver's situation.

  • Well-Weighted Pass: A pass that is accurate and has the correct pace for the receiver.

  • When to Press: Knowing the right moment to close down an opponent.

  • Whistle: The sound made by the referee to start, stop, or restart play.

  • Wide Play: Attacking down the flanks of the field.

  • Win the Ball: To successfully dispossess an opponent.

  • Wing: The wide areas of the field, near the sidelines.

  • Wing Play: Attacking strategy that focuses on play down the wings.

  • Winning Mentality: A player's or team's desire and belief in winning.

  • Winning Tackle: A successful tackle that dispossesses an opponent.

  • Wise Pass: An intelligent pass that considers the situation and options.

  • With the Turn: Receiving the ball and immediately turning with it.

  • Without the Ball: Player's actions and positioning when their team does not have possession.

  • Work Rate: The amount of effort a player puts in during a game or practice.

  • Wrap Around: A pass or move that goes around an opponent.

  • Wrap Around Pass: A pass played around a defender to a teammate.

  • X-Pass: A cross-field pass.

  • Y-Axis: The vertical dimension of the field, used for passing lanes or player movement.

  • Yard: A unit of measurement on the field.

  • Yellow Card: A caution given by the referee for a foul or misconduct.

  • Z-Axis: The depth dimension of the field.

  • Zonal Marking: Defending a specific area of the field rather than a particular opponent.

  • Zone: A designated area on the field.

Coach’s Troubleshooting:

  • Players confused by new terms: Integrate glossary terms into drills and games. Ask players to explain terms back. Use visual cues and demonstrations.
  • Glossary overwhelming: Focus on introducing 5-7 key terms per session, relevant to the week's tactical focus, and gradually add more.
  • Definitions too complex: Simplify language further, using analogies that U12 players can relate to. Ensure terms are explained in the context of their practical application on the field.

Age-Appropriate Language:

  • Use clear, concise definitions.
  • Relate terms to actions on the field (e.g., "when you see space, that's your opportunity to make a run").
  • Encourage players to ask questions and clarify meanings.
