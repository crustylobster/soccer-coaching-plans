U15 Soccer Glossary

Welcome to the U15 season! This glossary delves into advanced tactical and strategic concepts, emphasizing analytical thinking, leadership, and performance optimization. Understanding these terms will empower you to make sophisticated decisions, contribute effectively to team strategy, and develop your own game intelligently.

  • 1v1 Dominance: Successfully beating an opponent in a one-on-one situation through skill, speed, or deception.

  • 1v2 / 2v1 Situations: Understanding how to play effectively when outnumbered or having a numerical advantage in a small area.

  • Advanced Pressing Triggers: Identifying specific moments or actions by the opponent that initiate a team's pressing sequence.

  • Aerial Duels: Contesting for the ball when it is in the air, typically for headers.

  • Analyst Mindset: Approaching the game with a critical and observational perspective, seeking to understand patterns and strategies.

  • Anti-Press: Tactics used to break down an opponent's press and regain possession or build play.

  • Area Control: Dominating a specific zone on the field through positioning and intelligent play.

  • Asymmetrical Shape: A team's formation or player positioning being uneven, often used to exploit space or create specific attacking/defensive advantages.

  • Attacking Channel Play: Utilizing the "channels" or "half-spaces" between the center and the sidelines for attacking progression.

  • Attacking Third Exploitation: Strategies focused on creating and finishing chances in the final third of the pitch.

  • Augmented Reality (AR) Coaching: Using technology to overlay tactical information or feedback onto a live view of the game.

  • Back Line Penetration: Tactics aimed at breaking through the opponent's defensive line with passes or runs.

  • Balanced Attack: Ensuring offensive threats are spread effectively across the width and depth of the field.

  • Base Tactical Principles: Fundamental strategic ideas that guide a team's play.

  • Behavioral Analysis: Observing and understanding player or team tendencies.

  • Best Practice: Optimal methods or techniques for performing a specific skill or task.

  • Bicycle Kick: An acrobatic kick performed with the legs in a pedaling motion in mid-air.

  • Big Picture Thinking: Understanding the overall game strategy and how individual actions contribute to it.

  • Bilateral Attack: Attacking play that effectively utilizes both flanks of the field.

  • Bind: To draw opponents out of position, creating space elsewhere.

  • Blind Pass: A pass made without looking, relying on anticipation or deception.

  • Block Passing: Passing the ball between players in close proximity to maintain possession and advance play.

  • Block Shedding: A defender's action to disengage from an opponent and reposition effectively.

  • Block Transfer: Moving the ball between players in a connected sequence.

  • Blocking Passing Lanes: Positioning to intercept or disrupt opponent passes.

  • Blue Print: A pre-defined tactical plan or strategy.

  • Body Feint: Deceptive movement of the upper body to unbalance an opponent.

  • Body Positioning: How a player orients their body relative to the ball, teammates, and opponents.

  • Bold Play: Taking calculated risks to create significant advantages.

  • Bonding: Building team cohesion and trust.

  • Box Entry: The action of dribbling or passing the ball into the opponent's penalty area.

  • Box Play: Tactics specifically designed for attacking and defending within the penalty area.

  • Break Lines: To play a pass that bypasses one or more lines of the opponent's formation.

  • Break Press: Successfully playing through or around an opponent's pressing system.

  • Breakaway: A run into open space with a chance to score, often after winning possession.

  • Bridge Pass: A pass that connects different lines of play or players.

  • Build-Up Play: The process of moving the ball from defense to attack.

  • Bullet Pass: A fast, hard, and accurate pass.

  • Bypass: To get past an opponent with skill or movement.

  • Calibrate: To adjust strategy or actions based on game feedback.

  • Call-Off: A defensive instruction for a teammate to drop back, indicating the current defender will cover.

  • Call for the Ball: Verbal communication to request a pass.

  • Camouflage: Deceptive movements or positioning to mislead opponents.

  • Canvass: To survey or assess the field for options.

  • Captains' Role: Key players responsible for leadership and communication.

  • Carries: Dribbling the ball forward.

  • Carry the Play: Advancing the ball forward through dribbling.

  • Catalyst for Change: A player or action that initiates a tactical shift or momentum swing.

  • Center Circle Entry: The process of moving the ball into the center circle to initiate build-up.

  • Center-Back Play: Tactical responsibilities and skills of central defenders.

  • Central Midfield Dominance: Controlling the midfield area to dictate the game's tempo.

  • Channel Navigation: Skillful dribbling or passing through the "channels" between defenders.

  • Channel Runs: Runs made by players into the spaces between the center-backs and fullbacks.

  • Chaos Creation: Tactics designed to disrupt the opponent's organization and create unpredictable situations.

  • Check Shoulder: Glancing over the shoulder to assess the field before receiving or passing.

  • Check to the Ball: Moving towards the ball to receive a pass.

  • Championship Mentality: A mindset focused on winning and continuous improvement.

  • Chance Creation: Tactics and skills aimed at generating scoring opportunities.

  • Change of Pace: Accelerating quickly to beat an opponent or exploit space.

  • Change of Direction: Rapidly altering the path of movement while dribbling or running.

  • Channeling: Directing an opponent or the ball into a specific, often disadvantageous, area.

  • Channel Play: Attacking or defending through the "channels" of the field.

  • Channel Runs: Runs made by players into the space between the center-backs and fullbacks.

  • Character in Sport: Qualities like resilience, discipline, and sportsmanship.

  • Chart Analysis: Reviewing tactical diagrams or data to understand strategies.

  • Check of the Ball: A quick look around the field before receiving possession.

  • Check Over Shoulder: Scanning the field to see teammates and opponents.

  • Check to the Ball: Moving towards the ball to receive a pass.

  • Check Wing: Moving towards the sideline.

  • Check Wing-Back: Advancing or supporting along the flank.

  • Chemical Reaction: Metaphor for how players combine and react together.

  • Chess Match: A game where tactical maneuvering and strategic thinking are paramount.

  • Chicane: A sequence of sharp turns or feints.

  • Chief Playmaker: The player who orchestrates the team's attack.

  • Chip and Charge: A tactic involving a chip pass followed by a quick run into space.

  • Chip Pass: A softly lofted pass.

  • Chip Shot: A soft shot hit over the goalkeeper.

  • Chivvy: To encourage teammates.

  • Choice Architecture: Designing drills or game scenarios that encourage specific tactical decisions.

  • Choreographed Movement: Coordinated team movements practiced in training.

  • Christian Pulisic Move: A specific dribbling or feinting technique.

  • Chronic Injury: A long-term physical problem.

  • Chronological: Arranged in order of time.

  • Circulation: The movement of the ball between players.

  • Circumvention: Outmaneuvering an opponent or defensive structure.

  • Classic Formations: Traditional team shapes like 4-4-2 or 3-5-2.

  • Clean Sheet: Preventing the opponent from scoring.

  • Clearance: Kicking the ball away from danger.

  • Clever Play: Skillful and intelligent actions on the ball.

  • Climate of Trust: An environment where players feel safe to take risks and learn.

  • Clinical Finishing: Scoring goals with precision and efficiency.

  • Clip: A short video segment or a lofted pass.

  • Close Control Dribbling: Keeping the ball extremely close to the feet.

  • Close Marking: Defending an opponent very tightly.

  • Close Support: Positioning oneself near the ball carrier to offer a passing option.

  • Close-Down: To apply immediate pressure to the ball carrier.

  • Closeness: The proximity of players to each other or the ball.

  • Coaching Philosophy: The underlying beliefs and principles that guide a coach's approach.

  • Coaching Points: Specific instructions or feedback given during training.

  • Cohesion: The degree to which players work together as a unified team.

  • Cold Start: Beginning a practice or game without adequate warm-up.

  • Collective Defending: The entire team working together to defend.

  • Collective Intelligence: The combined game understanding of the entire team.

  • Collective Press: The entire team applying pressure on the opponent.

  • Collective Responsibility: Players sharing accountability for team performance.

  • Collective Tactical Awareness: The team's overall understanding of its strategy.

  • Collarbone Tackle: A tackle to the opponent's collarbone (illegal).

  • Combination Play: Two or more players passing back and forth to create an advantage.

  • Comfort Zone: A player's preferred or familiar area of play.

  • Command of the Ball: Exceptional skill in controlling and manipulating the ball.

  • Commanding Presence: A player's influential and authoritative demeanor on the field.

  • Communication Protocols: Established ways for players to communicate effectively.

  • Compact Defensive Block: A tightly organized defensive formation denying space.

  • Compact Midfield: Midfielders positioned close together to control the central area.

  • Compactness: The principle of players staying close to each other to deny space.

  • Compensation: Adjusting player positions or roles to cover for teammates.

  • Complex Transitions: Advanced tactical shifts between attack and defense.

  • Component Parts: The individual skills and tactical elements that make up the game.

  • Composition: The arrangement and positioning of players on the field.

  • Compound Move: A sequence of dribbling skills performed together.

  • Composure: Maintaining calmness and control under pressure.

  • Conceal: To hide the ball or one's intentions.

  • Concentration: Sustained mental focus.

  • Conceptual Understanding: Grasping the underlying ideas behind tactics.

  • Conditional Pressing: Applying pressure only when certain game conditions are met.

  • Conditioning: Improving players' physical fitness for soccer.

  • Cone Dribbling: Maneuvering the ball around cones.

  • Cone Gates: Using cones to create passing or dribbling channels.

  • Confidence: Belief in one's own abilities and decisions.

  • Connect Lines: To link players or units of the team through passing.

  • Connection: The link between players or team units.

  • Conscious Decision-Making: Making deliberate choices based on tactical understanding.

  • Consequence Awareness: Understanding the results of one's actions.

  • Consolidate: To establish a strong defensive shape or maintain possession.

  • Constant Pressure: Applying sustained pressure on the opponent.

  • Constructive Feedback: Advice given to help players improve.

  • Contain: To limit an opponent's progress without necessarily tackling.

  • Content: Satisfied with performance or outcome.

  • Continuous Improvement: The ongoing process of getting better.

  • Control of Tempo: Dictating the pace of the game.

  • Controlled First Touch: Receiving the ball cleanly and in a position to make an immediate next action.

  • Controlled Dribble: Dribbling the ball with precision and close attention.

  • Controller: A player who dictates the game's rhythm and flow.

  • Convergence: Players moving towards the same area of the field.

  • Convergent Attack: An attack that focuses play towards the center.

  • Conversion Rate: The percentage of chances that result in goals.

  • Conveyor Belt Play: Consistent and effective ball movement through passing.

  • Cooperative Play: Actions where players work together effectively.

  • Coordinate Movement: Players moving together in a planned and synchronized manner.

  • Corner Kick Strategy: Tactical approaches for attacking and defending corner kicks.

  • Corner Routines: Pre-planned attacking plays for corner kicks.

  • Corner Specialist: A player skilled at taking corner kicks.

  • Corridor of Play: A specific channel or area on the field being utilized.

  • Corridors of Uncertainty: Spaces in front of defenders that attackers aim to exploit.

  • Counter Attack: A rapid offensive move after winning possession.

  • Counter Press: Immediately applying pressure to win the ball back after losing possession.

  • Counter Movement: A run or action made in response to an opponent's movement or a shift in play.

  • Counter Play: Attacking after successfully defending.

  • Counter Threat: An opponent's ability to launch a quick attack.

  • Country-Specific Rules: Variations in rules or interpretations based on region (less common in soccer).

  • Court Vision: A player's ability to see and understand the entire playing field.

  • Cover: To position oneself behind a teammate to provide defensive support.

  • Covering the Space: Defenders positioning themselves to deny the opponent areas to play into.

  • Cow Corner: A specific position on the field, often near the corner flag.

  • Craft: Skillful and intelligent play.

  • Create Angle: To position oneself to provide a clear passing option.

  • Create Space: Players moving to open up passing lanes or areas for teammates.

  • Create the Chance: Actions taken to generate a scoring opportunity.

  • Creative Dribbling: Using skill and imagination to beat opponents.

  • Creative Play: Imaginative and unpredictable actions on the ball.

  • Creativity: The ability to produce novel and effective solutions or actions.

  • Credibility: Earning trust through competence and consistency.

  • Crossing Accuracy: Precision in delivering the ball into the penalty area from wide positions.

  • Crossing Angle: The angle from which a cross is delivered.

  • Crucial Pass: A pass that leads directly to a goal-scoring opportunity.

  • Cruyff Turn: A skillful dribbling move involving a feint and a pull-back.

  • Crystal Clear: Having perfect understanding of a tactical concept.

  • Cushion Control: Receiving the ball softly to absorb pace.

  • Cut Back: A pass played back inside from the byline towards the penalty area.

  • Cut Inside: Dribbling from a wide position towards the center of the field.

  • Cut Off Passing Lanes: Positioning to intercept or disrupt opponent passes.

  • Cut Out: To intercept a pass.

  • Cutting Angle: A defender moving to reduce space or passing options for an attacker.

  • Cutting Pass: A precise pass that splits defenders.

  • Cycle Play: Moving the ball between different lines or areas of the field.

  • Cycling: The continuous movement and passing of the ball within a team.

  • D-Box: Penalty Area.

  • D-Zone: Defensive zone or penalty area.

  • Dagger Pass: A sharp, incisive pass.

  • Danger Area: The space in front of the goal where scoring opportunities are created.

  • Danger Zone: The penalty area or other areas of high attacking threat.

  • Dangerous Play: Actions that could cause injury or risk.

  • Dark Art: Cunning or less ethical tactics used to gain an advantage.

  • Dead Ball: A restart in play (free kick, corner, throw-in).

  • Dead Ball Specialist: A player skilled at taking free kicks or corners.

  • Decision Tree: A framework for making tactical choices based on different scenarios.

  • Decision Matrix: A table outlining tactical options and their likely outcomes.

  • Decision Window: The short period of time available to make a tactical choice.

  • Deep Playmaker: A midfielder who operates in a deep position to control the team's build-up.

  • Deep Defensive Line: When the defensive line plays far back.

  • Deep Runs: Runs made by players from deeper positions into attacking areas.

  • Deep-lying Forward: A forward player who drops deeper to link play.

  • Deep-lying Midfielder: A midfielder who plays in a defensive role, linking defense and attack.

  • Defend the Box: Protecting the penalty area from opposition attacks.

  • Defend the Space: Positioning to deny opponents areas to play into.

  • Defender's Angle: The angle a defender takes when approaching an attacker.

  • Defensive Awareness: Understanding defensive responsibilities and positioning.

  • Defensive Block: A compact line or group of defenders.

  • Defensive Coverage: Players positioning themselves to support teammates defensively.

  • Defensive Discipline: Maintaining shape and focus during defensive phases.

  • Defensive Footwork: The specific movements and stances used when defending.

  • Defensive Midfielder: A player who operates in front of the defense to break up attacks.

  • Defensive Organization: How well a team is structured when defending.

  • Defensive Phase: The period when a team is without possession and focused on defending.

  • Defensive Pressure: Applying pressure to the ball carrier to force errors or regain possession.

  • Defensive Recovery: The process of reorganizing defensively after losing possession.

  • Defensive Shape: The formation and relative positions of players when defending.

  • Defensive Solidity: How strong and difficult a team is to break down defensively.

  • Defensive Transition: The moment a team loses possession and must switch to defending.

  • Defensive Third: The area of the pitch closest to a team's own goal.

  • Deflection: A change in the ball's direction after hitting a player.

  • Delaying Tactics: Actions taken to slow down an opponent's attack and allow teammates to recover.

  • Deliberate Play: Actions taken with clear tactical intent.

  • Delivery: The act of passing or crossing the ball.

  • Delivery Accuracy: Precision in passing or crossing.

  • Delivery Technique: The method used to pass or cross the ball.

  • Demarcation: Marking boundaries on the field for drills.

  • Demarcated Area: A clearly defined playing space.

  • Demanding: Requiring high effort or focus.

  • Demonstration: Showing players how to perform a skill or tactic.

  • Depth: The distance players cover vertically on the field, from defense to attack.

  • Depth Runs: Runs made into the "depth" of the opponent's half or behind the defensive line.

  • Derby Match: A game between two local rivals.

  • Descending Ball: A ball that is falling from the air.

  • Descriptive Analysis: Explaining tactical situations in detail.

  • Destructive Play: Tactics focused on breaking up opponent attacks.

  • Development Phase: The stage of a player's or team's growth.

  • Diagonal Pass: A pass played across the field at an angle.

  • Diagonal Movement: Moving across the field at an angle.

  • Diagonal Run: A run made across the field at an angle.

  • Diagrammatic Analysis: Using visual aids to explain tactics.

  • Diegetic Sound: Sounds that originate from within the game itself (e.g., ball hit, whistle).

  • Difference Maker: A player who significantly impacts the game outcome.

  • Difficult Pass: A pass that is challenging to execute accurately.

  • Direct Play: Playing the ball forward quickly, often with long passes.

  • Direct Pressure: Immediately closing down the ball carrier.

  • Direct Shot: A shot taken immediately upon receiving the ball.

  • Direct Tactics: Strategies that focus on quickly advancing the ball.

  • Directional Change: Altering the path of movement.

  • Disguise: Concealing the intention of a pass or move.

  • Disguised Pass: A pass that appears to be going in one direction but is played elsewhere.

  • Dislodge: To move an opponent out of their position.

  • Disorganisation: Lack of structure or coordination.

  • Disrupt: To break up the opponent's rhythm or play.

  • Distance Covered: The total ground a player or team travels during a game.

  • Distance on the Ball: Keeping the ball close while dribbling.

  • Distance to Target: The space between the player and their intended pass or shot.

  • Distraction: Anything that diverts a player's focus.

  • Distribution: How a player passes or distributes the ball.

  • Distribution of Players: The arrangement of players on the field.

  • Distraction Tactics: Actions intended to disrupt an opponent's concentration.

  • Diving Header: A header performed while diving forward.

  • Diving Save: A goalkeeper's save made by diving.

  • Doggedness: Tenacity and persistence in play.

  • Dominance: Controlling the game or a specific area of the field.

  • Dominant Play: A style of play that dictates the game's flow.

  • Donkey Pass: A poorly executed pass.

  • Doorstep Save: A save made close to the goal line.

  • Double Pivot: Two defensive midfielders operating side-by-side.

  • Double Team: Two players marking the same opponent.

  • Double Touch: A dribbling move involving two touches of the ball.

  • Doubling the Fullback: A winger cutting inside from a wide position.

  • Down the Line: Passing or dribbling towards the sideline.

  • Down the Throat: A shot directly at the goalkeeper.

  • Draggable Cones: Cones that can be easily moved to change drill setups.

  • Drag Back: A dribbling skill where the ball is pulled back with the sole of the foot.

  • Drag to the Ball: Moving the ball with the foot to change its direction.

  • Drag the Ball: Pulling the ball back with the foot.

  • Drag Turn: A turn executed by pulling the ball back.

  • Dragon Fly: A fancy flick or skill.

  • Dribble "in the pocket": Dribbling in an area of space with no immediate pressure.

  • Dribble Past: Successfully beating an opponent with a dribble.

  • Dribble Through: Maneuvering the ball through a gap or around obstacles.

  • Dribble Width: Keeping the ball wider when dribbling to cover more ground.

  • Dribbling: Running with the ball at one's feet.

  • Drill: A specific practice exercise.

  • Drive: To dribble forward with speed and power.

  • Drive Pass: A hard, driven pass.

  • Drive Run: A strong, purposeful run.

  • Drop Deep: To move backward into a more defensive position.

  • Drop Pass: A pass played backward to a teammate.

  • Drop Shoulder: A feint where a player drops their shoulder as if moving in one direction.

  • Drop Shoulder Turn: A skill move involving a shoulder drop and a subsequent turn.

  • Drop the Ball: To receive the ball and trap it softly.

  • Dropping Off: Moving backward or away from a player or area.

  • Drop Off Pass: A pass played to a teammate who is dropping deeper.

  • Drop Shoulder Feint: A deceptive movement of the shoulder.

  • Drop Shoulder Turn: A turn using a shoulder feint.

  • Drop Zone: An area on the field designated for specific plays or player positions.

  • Drop Your Shoulder: A feinting technique.

  • Ducking Inside: Cutting inside from a wide position.

  • Dual Threat: A player who is effective in both attack and defense.

  • Dummy Run: A run made to draw defenders away, creating space for a teammate.

  • Dummy the Ball: To feint or pretend to play the ball.

  • Dummy Time: Time spent practicing skills without a ball.

  • Dummy Pass: A feigned pass.

  • Duo Play: Two players combining effectively.

  • Duplicate Movements: Repeating specific patterns of play.

  • Duplicate Runs: Players making similar runs.

  • Duration of Play: The length of time the ball is in active play.

  • Duration of Possession: The length of time a team maintains possession.

  • Durability: A player's ability to withstand physical challenges and maintain performance.

  • Dynamic Movement: Quick, agile, and varied movements.

  • Dynamic Warm-up: Active movements to prepare the body for exercise.

  • Dynamic Positioning: Adjusting player positions fluidly based on game flow.

  • Dynamic Support: Players making runs to support the ball carrier effectively.

  • Dynamic Warm-up: Engaging in active movements to prepare the body for play.

  • E-Pass: A type of pass, possibly referring to a specific trajectory or purpose.

  • Each Way Bet: A metaphor for a balanced approach to offense and defense.

  • Early Pressure: Applying pressure to the opponent soon after losing possession.

  • Early Support: Players positioning themselves to offer quick support.

  • Earn the Ball: To win possession through skillful play or defense.

  • Earned Goal: A goal scored through good play and effort.

  • Easy Pass: A simple, accurate pass.

  • Easy Decision: A straightforward choice in a game situation.

  • Easy Possession: Maintaining control of the ball with little difficulty.

  • Easy Save: A save made by the goalkeeper with minimal effort.

  • Echelon Runs: Runs made by teammates at different angles and depths to offer passing options.

  • Echo Pass: A return pass played back to the passer.

  • Effective Pressure: Pressure applied in a way that is likely to win the ball or force an error.

  • Effective Transition: Quickly and efficiently switching between defensive and attacking phases.

  • Effective Use of Space: Utilizing open areas on the field to one's advantage.

  • Efficiency: Performing actions with minimal wasted effort.

  • Efficient Play: Using the ball and energy wisely to achieve objectives.

  • Effort: The exertion of physical or mental energy.

  • Eighty-Yard Run: A long run covering a significant distance on the field.

  • Elbow Pass: An illegal pass using the elbow.

  • Elbow Touch: Using the elbow to control the ball (illegal).

  • Elevation: The height of a kicked ball or a player's jump.

  • Eliminate: To beat an opponent with skill.

  • Elongated Possession: Maintaining control of the ball for an extended period.

  • Elongated Play: A long period of sustained action.

  • Emergent Tactics: Strategies that develop spontaneously during a game.

  • Emulate: To imitate or copy the style of a player or team.

  • End Line: The boundary line at the back of the pitch.

  • End Zone: The area near the goal where scoring opportunities are created.

  • End-to-End Play: Fast-paced action that moves quickly between both ends of the field.

  • End-to-End Transition: Rapid movement of play from one penalty box to the other.

  • Endurance: The ability to sustain physical or mental effort over time.

  • Energy Management: Conserving and expending energy strategically during a game.

  • Energy Output: The level of physical exertion a player exerts.

  • Energy System: The body's physiological systems for producing energy.

  • Engage: To close down an opponent or challenge for the ball.

  • Engagement: The act of challenging for the ball or applying pressure.

  • Engine Room: The midfield area, often seen as the heart of the team.

  • Enveloping Run: A run made around a teammate with the ball to offer a forward passing option.

  • Envelopment: The action of surrounding or covering an opponent.

  • Equalizer: A goal that levels the score.

  • Equalizing Goal: A goal that makes the score even.

  • Equilibrium: A state of balance in the game.

  • Equipment Check: Ensuring players have the correct gear.

  • Erase: To negate an opponent's threat or advantage.

  • Error: A mistake made by a player or team.

  • Error Correction: Learning from and fixing mistakes.

  • Error Minimization: Strategies to reduce the frequency of mistakes.

  • Error Reduction: Improving accuracy and decision-making to make fewer errors.

  • Error Tolerance: A team's ability to continue playing effectively despite individual errors.

  • Escalate the Tempo: To increase the pace of the game.

  • Establish Possession: To gain and maintain control of the ball.

  • Establish Shape: To adopt the correct team formation or defensive structure.

  • Etiquette: Proper conduct and sportsmanship.

  • Evade: To get past an opponent with skill.

  • Evading: The act of avoiding opponents or challenges.

  • Even Numbers: When both teams have the same number of players in an area or in the game.

  • Even Press: Applying pressure symmetrically across the field.

  • Evergreen Skills: Fundamental soccer skills that remain important at all levels.

  • Every Angle: Considering all possible passing or attacking options.

  • Everywhere Player: A player who is highly active and involved all over the pitch.

  • Exact Pass: A pass delivered with precise accuracy and weight.

  • Exacting: Requiring high levels of skill and precision.

  • Exaggerate: To overdo a movement, often as a feint.

  • Exaltation: Great joy, often after scoring a goal.

  • Excellence: Performing at a very high standard.

  • Exchange: A quick pass between two players.

  • Excite: To generate enthusiasm and energy.

  • Execute: To perform a skill or tactic correctly.

  • Execution: The act of performing a skill or tactic.

  • Exit: To move away from a situation or player.

  • Exit Pass: A pass made to move the ball out of a crowded or dangerous area.

  • Exit Strategy: A plan for how to transition out of a specific game situation.

  • Exo-skeletal: Describing a rigid or predictable team shape.

  • Exotic Skill: An unusual or highly advanced technical move.

  • Expectation Management: Setting realistic goals and understanding outcomes.

  • Expected Goals (xG): A statistical measure of the probability of scoring from a given shot.

  • Experience: Knowledge and skill gained through playing.

  • Experiment: Trying new tactics or skills.

  • Expert Play: Performing skills and tactics at a high level of proficiency.

  • Explain: To make something clear or understandable.

  • Explode: To accelerate quickly.

  • Explode into Space: To make a sudden, fast run into an open area.

  • Exploitation: Taking advantage of an opponent's weakness or an opportunity.

  • Exploit Space: To use open areas on the field to one's advantage.

  • Exploit Width: Using the sidelines to stretch the opponent's defense.

  • Exploit Weaknesses: Targeting opponent vulnerabilities.

  • Exploratory Play: Trying different approaches to find what works.

  • Explosive Power: The ability to generate rapid force.

  • Exposed: Left vulnerable or open to attack.

  • Extended Support: Players positioning themselves further away to offer longer passing options.

  • Extension: A player's reach or the way they use their body with the ball.

  • External Factors: Influences outside the direct control of the team (e.g., weather).

  • External Pressure: Pressure from outside sources (e.g., crowd).

  • Extra Man: Having a numerical advantage in a specific area.

  • Eye Contact: Making eye contact with a teammate before passing to them.

  • Eye for Goal: Skill in finding and hitting the target.

  • Eye for the Pass: Skill in identifying and executing the correct pass.

  • Eyes Focused: Maintaining concentration on the ball and surroundings.

  • Eyes Up: A reminder for players to scan the field for options.
